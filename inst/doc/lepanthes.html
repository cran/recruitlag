The Lepanthes eltoroensis analysis

library(recruitlag)
library(dplyr)

This vignette reproduces the analysis of the companion paper on the epiphytic orchid Lepanthes eltoroensis (Luquillo Mountains, Puerto Rico), in which reproduction is continuous in time and recruitment is episodic. The package’s tests check the numbers below against the archived outputs of the paper’s pipeline; here they are simply computed.

The monthly survey (1994 to 1996) counts inflorescence-bearing adults and new seedlings at 23 monthly censuses of four populations; recruits are defined by first appearance, so they are scored from census 2. The six-monthly survey (1999 to 2004) counts every plant on 23 host trees at 12 censuses; recruits are scored at censuses 3 to 12. lepanthes_census holds its system totals and lepanthes_hosts one row per host tree and census, which is the input of the host-level test.

The lag conventions differ, and the package makes them explicit. The monthly lag model of the paper reads lag k at month t - k over a six-month horizon (K = 6, lag0 = 0). The six-monthly model reads lag k at census t - 1 - k over five six-month bins (K = 4, lag0 = 1). The bins are labelled 0-6, 6-12, 12-18, 18-24 and 24-30 months, counted from the first lagged census, which is the convention of the kernel labels throughout.

At both time scales the ceiling is low because the input is flat, the searched maximum is attained by a pure delay and lies within 0.01 of the theorem bound (the small difference arises from backfilling pre-record lags), and observed recruitment exceeds the ceiling by a factor of about 9 (monthly) and 2.6 (six-monthly) on the Gini.

What any lag profile predicts

profiles <- list(

  `evenly spread (0 to 30 months)` = rep(.2, 5),

  `fitted posterior mean` = lepanthes_profile$weight)
rain_profiles(profiles, bin = 6, unit = "months")

rain_expected(c_6, profiles)

The expected series all but coincide and follow the slow decline of reproduction; none resembles the recruits counted (62 at census 5, none at censuses 7, 8 and 12, 32 at census 10). The fitted posterior-mean profile from the paper’s distributed-lag model is among them and is indistinguishable from the rest, which is the identification problem the paper describes: with a flat input the lag cannot be recovered, however long the series.

How firm the exceedance is

The ceiling is arithmetic and carries no uncertainty. The two concentration indices it is compared with are estimates from ten censuses, and so is their ratio, and a reader is entitled to an interval on them. The package gives three, because they answer differently worded questions and a user should take the one whose logic they accept.

The first resamples the 23 host trees with replacement, rebuilds both series from the resampled trees, and recomputes the ceiling and the exceedance in each replicate. It is a confidence interval, BCa by default, and is read in the usual way. The share of replicates at or below 1 is reported as what it is, a one-sided bootstrap p-value, and not as a probability that the exceedance is at most 1.

set.seed(20260913)
k4 <- lag_kernels(4, bin = 6, unit = "mo")
lag_ceiling_boot(lepanthes_hosts, unit = "host", reproduction = "inflorescences",
                 K = 4, kernels = k4, R = 2000)
#> Cluster bootstrap of the ceiling: 23 units resampled 2000 times, 90% BCa intervals
#>   estimand: realised counts; K = 4, lag0 = 1, 20 kernels
#>   observed Gini     0.649   [0.578, 0.694]
#>   ceiling Gini      0.254   [0.196, 0.291]
#>   exceedance Gini   2.56   [2.11, 3.18]
#>   observed CV       1.377   [1.143, 1.598]
#>   ceiling CV        0.547   [0.426, 0.637]
#>   exceedance CV     2.51   [1.94, 3.27]
#>   one-sided bootstrap p-value for exceedance <= 1: Gini < 5e-04, CV < 5e-04
#>   A confidence interval, read in the usual way; p_boot is not a posterior probability.

The second is Rubin’s Bayesian bootstrap: Dirichlet weights over the hosts in place of resampling, and a posterior in place of a sampling distribution. The numbers are close to the first, which is expected; the difference is that P(exceedance > 1) is now a posterior probability under a stated prior and may be written as one.

lag_ceiling_bayesboot(lepanthes_hosts, unit = "host", reproduction = "inflorescences",
                      K = 4, kernels = k4, draws = 4000)
#> Bayesian bootstrap of the ceiling: 23 units, 4000 posterior draws, 90% equal-tailed credible intervals
#>   estimand: realised counts; K = 4, lag0 = 1, 20 kernels
#>   observed Gini     0.649   [0.609, 0.731]
#>   ceiling Gini      0.254   [0.222, 0.318]
#>   exceedance Gini   2.56   [2.06, 3.07]
#>   observed CV       1.377   [1.228, 1.809]
#>   ceiling CV        0.547   [0.462, 0.669]
#>   exceedance CV     2.51   [2.04, 3.46]
#>   P(exceedance > 1): Gini > 0.9998, CV > 0.9998

The third fits a negative-binomial model to the counts on every host at every census, with host and census effects, and puts the posterior of the expected series through the same ceiling arithmetic. That is the object the bound applies to, and its exceedance is lower than the raw one, because part of the concentration of small counts is sampling variation about a smoother mean. It needs cmdstanr and CmdStan, so it is not run here.

lag_ceiling_stan(lepanthes_hosts, unit = "host", reproduction = "inflorescences",
                 K = 4, kernels = k4)

About a mean that obeys the ceiling, negative-binomial counts with the observed variance-to-mean ratio produce a series as concentrated as the observed one roughly one time in 3. The exceedance of the ceiling by the pooled counts is therefore not evidence; the test has to be made across host trees.

Three of the ten censuses are silent (no recruit on any of the 23 hosts) and one carries 62. Under the lag hypothesis, expected recruitment on each host is that host’s own lagged inflorescence record, scaled to its own total. The kernels are the pure delays, every uniform window, geometric decay and the fitted profile; the paper adds 800 Dirichlet draws, which take longer and change nothing.

set.seed(20260910)
ht <- host_lag_test(lepanthes_hosts, unit = "host", reproduction = "inflorescences",
                    K = 4, nsim = 4000,
                    kernels = lag_kernels(4, bin = 6, unit = "mo",
                                          extra = list(`fitted posterior mean` = lepanthes_profile$weight)),
                    phi = 0.089)
ht

The moment estimate of the clumping parameter under the flat reference is about 0.19; the paper also evaluates every profile at 0.089, the estimate obtained about a single pooled mean, which attributes the between-host spread of the means to clumping and is about twice the clumping the counts support. Even at that value the most favourable profile does not make three silent censuses ordinary.

No lag profile, of any shape over the 30-month window, makes the observed record ordinary. Recruitment in this population is not the delayed consequence of its own reproduction; something else opens and closes.
